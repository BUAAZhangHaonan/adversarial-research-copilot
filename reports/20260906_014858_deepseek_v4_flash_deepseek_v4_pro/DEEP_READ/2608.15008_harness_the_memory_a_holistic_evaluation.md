# Harness the Memory: A Holistic Evaluation of Memory Substrates in Memory Agents

- arxiv: 2608.15008
- venue: None

## Extraction

This paper is **directly relevant** to the research question: it is a controlled multi-benchmark, multi-backbone evaluation of memory substrates for LLM agents, and it makes explicit methodological, scalability, and routing claims. Below, each section distinguishes **[author-stated]** content from **[my inference]**.

## (1) VERIFIED CLAIMS

### What was actually demonstrated — and under which conditions

**[Author-stated] A controlled substrate-isolation experiment was run.**
Per Section 3 ("Experiment Design"), the memory substrate was treated as the sole manipulated variable: "isolate the implemented memory substrate family under a shared harness, backbone, prompting, and auxiliary-LLM setting." Conditions were:

- **Substrates:** 11 methods (M1–M11) spanning seven substrate families — Flat Index, Text Record, Structural, Hierarchical, Refinement, Weight (internal), Activation (internal) (Figure 2; Section 2; Table 4).
- **Backbones:** QWEN3-8B, QWEN3-32B-AWQ, GEMMA-4-26B-A4B-IT, served with vLLM on 4×H200 GPUs.
- **Auxiliary LLM fixed:** GPT-4O-MINI for all write/management/organization calls; GPT-4O-MINI also used as LLM judge with a shared judging template (Section 3, "Models, inference, and metrics").
- **Evaluation protocol:** identical interaction histories across substrates; 26 metrics spanning performance (P1–P12) and efficiency (E1–E15), including benchmark-native primary metrics (Task Success Rate/TSR on ALFWorld, Pass@1 on BigCodeBench-Hard, SubEM on MAB) (Table 6; Section 3).
- **Benchmarks / data distribution / scale:**
  - **User-centric QA:** LoCoMo (10 multi-session dialogues, 1,986 questions, five categories); MemoryAgentBench (MAB) capabilities LRU/TTL/CR plus LME-S (the AR subset measured on LongMemEval-S\*, 300 queries, ~355K tokens each) (Sections 3, C.1).
  - **Agent-centric decision-making:** ALFWorld (134 valid-unseen embodied-planning tasks) and BigCodeBench-Hard (148 code tasks, real PYTEST grading) (Section 3).
  - **Scalability probe:** MAB Conflict Resolution under 6K / 32K / 262K token conversation-length regimes, with question pool, gold answers, and judge held constant (Section 3, "Ablations"; Figure 6).
- **Ablation controls:** a top-k retrieval sweep on LoCoMo (k ∈ {1,2,5,10,20}) and ALFWorld (k ∈ {1,2,3,4,5}), with a **NoMem baseline** receiving the same template with an empty retrieved block "so the curves attribute quality to retrieval rather than scaffolding" (Section 3).
- **Exclusions by design:** M7 only on trajectory-bearing agentic tasks; M9 excluded from the GEMMA-4-A4B-MoE band; M10 (full context) omitted from both agentic benchmarks because cumulative context exceeds tested context windows (Section 3).

Given those conditions, the authors demonstrate:

**[Author-stated] No single substrate dominates; the optimal substrate reverses between regimes.**
- On LoCoMo and LME-S, **M5 (Dual-Level Graph / LightRAG-style structural substrate) performs best across all three model bands** (e.g., LoCoMo P4: 0.648 / 0.683 / 0.719 for QWEN3-8B / QWEN3-32B-AWQ / GEMMA-4; LME-S P4: 0.537 / 0.627 / 0.667) (Table 1; Section 4.1).
- On MAB sub-capabilities the leader changes: M10 leads LRU on QWEN3-8B, M3 leads LRU on QWEN3-32B-AWQ; M3 and M8 dominate TTL; M5 leads CR on two of three models (Table 1; Section 4.1).
- On **ALFWorld**, the winning substrates are denoising/abstraction-based rather than retrieval-maximizing: M11 doubles NoMem on QWEN3-8B (11.9 TSR vs 5.7), and M7 (Distilled Strategies) achieves the table's peak TSR of 32.1% on QWEN3-32B-AWQ vs 22.4% NoMem (Table 2; Section 4.2). **Importantly, the same M7 is not the winner on QWEN3-8B (7.5, below M11's 11.9) or GEMMA-4 (8.2, near NoMem's 7.5)** (Table 2) — the "refinement wins embodied planning" statement is thus backbone-conditioned.
- M5, although the QA leader, is not beneficial on agentic tasks and sits below NoMem (4.5 TSR) on GEMMA-4 ALFWorld (Table 2).

**[Author-stated] Efficiency/latency surfaces are disjoint across regimes and cost is often unrewarded.**
On QWEN3-32B-AWQ, the QA Pareto frontier is occupied by structural/hierarchical substrates (M5, M6) while the agentic frontier is occupied by disjoint substrates — flat retrieval M2 on BigCodeBench-Hard (+2.0% at 1.28× latency overhead) and refinement M7 on ALFWorld (+9.7% at 1.23× overhead) — "the two frontiers share no substrate" (Section 5.1, Figure 3). Heavy costs frequently buy nothing: e.g., on LME-S, M4 at 234 s underperforms M2 at 7.3 s (Section 5.1; Table 1 E15 columns show M4 LME-S E15 ≈ 225.7–234.0 s across backbones vs 1.75–8.49 s for M2/M1).

**[Author-stated] Retrieval breadth (k) has opposite effects in the two regimes** (Figure 4; Section 5.2).
- On LoCoMo (QWEN3-8B), P4 rises monotonically with k across substrates (k 1→20).
- On ALFWorld (QWEN3-32B-AWQ), task success falls as k grows: "M7 drops from 32.1% at k=1 to ~25% at k=5, while the flat retrievers M1 and M2 fall below the no-memory baseline of 22.4%", and steps-to-goal climbs toward the cap.
- The authors attribute this to **attention dilution with regime-dependent consequences**, based on an attention probe (Figure 5): growing k funnels attention mass out of task Context into Retrieved on both tasks (LoCoMo Context 0.34→0.10 vs Retrieved 0.05→0.66; ALFWorld Context 0.19→0.17 vs Retrieved 0.34→0.44). "The mechanism is identical, what differs is which region carries the answer": in QA the answer is in the retrieved block; in ALFWorld the load-bearing region is the current observation/admissible-action list, and Cue mass loses ~4% between k=1 and k=5 (Section 5.2).

**[Author-stated] Scalability introduces a third routing axis** (Figure 6; Section 5.3).
On MAB CR (QWEN3-32B-AWQ, 6K→262K tokens):
- Most external substrates improve in quality with scale — M8 (Skill Bundles) climbs 0.32→0.51; M5 climbs 0.28→0.48.
- Refinement substrates pair quality scaling with moderate latency growth (M8 ~1 s→~10 s) because stored bundles are fixed-size.
- Structural substrates (M4, M5) pay steep latency at 262K as graph rebuild/entity extraction scale with input size.
- Internal M10 scales in quality (0.27→0.47) but linearly in read cost; M9 (adapter weights) stays flat at 0.18–0.22.

**[Author-stated] Landscape/motivation claims** (Section 1, Figure 1, Appendix A): Across 52 memory-augmented systems (2023–2026), 62% of benchmark usage concentrates on LoCoMo + LongMemEval; only 21% report any efficiency metric; 81% use GPT-family backbones; no system simultaneously achieves broad benchmark coverage and comprehensive efficiency reporting. The survey's inclusion criteria and per-system table are in Appendix A.1/A.2/Table 3.

**[Author-stated] Semantic validity of the retrieval banks was checked** (Appendix C.2): ALFWorld test goals have nearest-bank cosine mean 0.879 vs 0.068 for a random Gaussian null; BCB-Hard mean 0.594 vs 0.061 random. This supports that the agentic evaluations measure use of relevant stored signal rather than retrieval against unrelated content.

### Which alternative explanations the evaluation does NOT rule out

**[My inference] — each is grounded in the paper's own scope/design:**

- **Backbone sampling is narrow and may interact with substrate conclusions.** Only three open-weight backbones are tested (two Qwen, one Gemma); 81% of the field's systems use GPT-family models (Section 1), which the study does not cover. The paper itself shows backbone-dependent reversals — e.g., raw-text retrieval on ALFWorld "is backbone-dependent rather than universally harmful" (Section 4.2), and M11 exhibits inverse scaling between QWEN3-8B and QWEN3-32B-AWQ (Appendix E.1). A different backbone sample could shift "no single substrate dominates" into "substrate X dominates on family Y."
- **No confidence intervals or significance tests are reported.** The authors state gaps ≥0.05 on P4 or task success are "unlikely to reverse under resampling" but present only point estimates (Appendix D.4). Some headline differences involve small absolute episode counts — e.g., on QWEN3-8B ALFWorld the spread from 3.0 (M9) to 11.9 (M11) TSR corresponds to ≈4–16 of 134 episodes — so rankings near the bottom could be noisy.
- **The attention explanation is correlational and narrowly measured.** The probe uses one backbone (QWEN3-8B), one substrate (M1), last-prompt-token eager attention in upper-half layers, head-summed, with hand-defined context bins (Section 5.2, Figure 5). This supports but does not establish a causal mechanism by which retrieved tokens "starve" policy-critical attention. A causal intervention (e.g., attention masking) is not performed.
- **The auxiliary LLM and judge are each a single model (GPT-4O-MINI).** Substrate write quality (graph extraction for M5, note evolution for M4, distillation for M7/M8) and P4 scoring both depend on this one model. The evaluation does not rule out that a stronger/weaker auxiliary model would reorder substrates, especially write-intensive structural/refinement ones.
- **LLM-as-a-judge may correlate with output style, not just correctness.** By design, the judge sees only (q, y, ŷ), never the retrieved evidence or memory trace (Appendix D.2, P4 definition). The evaluation does not rule out judge bias toward substrates that produce longer or differently-phrased answers.
- **Substrate implementations are approximations, so results do not strictly test the production systems they stand for.** M8 replaces MemSkill's PPO-trained read controller with a zero-shot LLM prefill of all bundles; M11 replaces EpiCache's in-place KV-cache edits with utterance-level re-prefill (Appendix G). Production systems Mem0, Zep, and MemGPT are excluded and only "loosely approximated" by M3/M5 (Appendix B.3). Thus the rankings strictly concern the paper's re-implementations.
- **The user-centric vs agent-centric contrast is confounded with benchmark/method specifics.** QA benchmarks use P4/LLM-judge/SubEM over multi-session dialogue; agentic benchmarks use TSR/Pass@1 over embodied/code tasks. Different metric noise properties, prompt formats, and task distributions could contribute to the observed "regime reversal" beyond the substrates themselves.
- **M10 (full context) is never tested on agentic tasks** (Section 3), so the claim that excessive context harms sequential decision-making is not directly demonstrated for the full-context substrate; it is inferred from k-sweeps of retrieval substrates.
- **Scalability evidence is limited to one benchmark configuration (MAB CR) and one backbone** (QWEN3-32B-AWQ) at three length points (6K/32K/262K) (Section 5.3, Figure 6). Extrapolation to other task types, other conflict dynamics, and longer-than-262K horizons is untested.
- **One-off construction costs skew latency comparisons.** For M6, tree construction is deferred to the first read and absorbed by the first query; "its steady-state per-query cost is comparable to the Flat baselines" (Section 5.1 and Appendix E.1), meaning the plotted Pareto position of M6 partially reflects an implementation artifact.

## (2) AUTHOR-STATED LIMITATIONS

The paper's explicit Limitations section (Appendix G) contains two limitations, plus several design-exclusion caveats stated elsewhere.

1. **Excluded production-grade systems (Appendix G, "Excluded production-grade systems"; supporting cost data in Appendix B.3, Table 5):** "We exclude three widely cited production-grade memory systems, MemGPT, Mem0, and Zep, from the main cross-substrate comparison because their auxiliary-LLM cost (2,700–9,000 calls and 11–23 hours per LoCoMo run) is one to two orders of magnitude above the lighter substrates, which would dominate the wall-clock and token-count columns and obscure architectural differences. Their core retrieval ideas are already represented in the main comparison by lighter, paper-faithful substrates (M3, M5)." The authors further concede in Appendix B.3 that these are "loose approximations" (e.g., "Mem0's per-input text distillation is loosely approximated by M3; Zep's temporal knowledge graph overlaps with the entity/relation indexing in M5"), and assert that inclusion "would not change any cross-substrate ranking reported in the main text" — an assertion, not a measurement.

2. **Implementation deviations from reference papers (Appendix G, "Implementation deviations from reference papers"):**
   - "M8 replaces MemSkill's PPO-trained controller with a zero-shot LLM that consumes all refined skill bundles prefilled into context, because no other substrate involves RL training and keeping M8 RL-driven would confound the cross-substrate comparison."
   - "M11 replaces EpiCache's in-place KV-cache edits with utterance-level re-prefill, because Qwen3's hybrid attention architecture is not supported by the authors' released kernels."
   - The authors state both deviations "preserve the core design principle of each method" but acknowledge they differ from the published implementations.

3. **Stated design exclusions (Section 3, "Experiment Design"):** "Three configurations are excluded by design: M7 only applies to trajectory-bearing agent-centric tasks... M9 is incompatible with the A4B MoE routing in GEMMA-4-26B-A4B-IT, and M10 (full context) is omitted from both agent-centric benchmarks (ALFWorld and BigCodeBench-Hard) because the cumulative context exceeds every tested context window." Appendix E.3 adds the operational rationale for M10 on ALFWorld: naive concatenation of trajectories exceeds 128K tokens by task 30–40, so "any reported M10 number on ALFWorld would reflect a truncation policy rather than the substrate."

4. **Reporting limitation (Appendix D.4, "Confidence intervals"):** "We report point estimates in the main tables for legibility. Because the benchmark sample sizes are large... the cross-substrate gaps we highlight (≥ 0.05 absolute on P4 or task success) are unlikely to reverse under resampling." Thus no formal intervals or tests are provided.

5. **Undefined-metric handling (Appendix D.4):** For substrates without retrieval (M10, M9), P6/E4 are undefined, and for embedding-only substrates E5/E6/E10/E11 are zero by construction; the authors "render undefined cells as 'n/a'... rather than imputing zeros, since a zero would bias direction-aware aggregates."

## (3) OPENED QUESTIONS

### Future work the authors call for (explicitly or by direct implication)

**[Author-stated]** There is no dedicated "Future Work" section, but the conclusion and synthesis sections make two programmatic calls:

1. **Universal agent memory must be a composed, routed multi-substrate (or multi-agent) system.** Repeated across the paper: "no single substrate can serve every regime, so universal agent memory must be realized as a multi-substrate or multi-agent system in which heterogeneous substrates are composed and routed per regime, each handling the operating conditions it is suited for" (Section 5, "NO SUBSTRATE WINS ALONE…"; Conclusion). The suggested division of labor: "refinement substrates curate abstractions, structural and text substrates hold facts, activation and flat substrates preserve raw experience — with the harness routing each query to the substrate whose representation aligns with the load-bearing region of its prompt" (Section 5).

2. **Routing decisions must be regime-conditioned along the three discovered axes.** Specifically: (a) retrieval breadth "is not a universal hyperparameter but a regime-conditioned one: the routing decision must be informed by whether the prompt's answer lives in retrieved memory or in the task context window" (Section 5.2); and (b) "Universal memory must route on history depth, not only on the current query" (Section 5.3). The abstract frames the paper's contribution as providing "empirical guidance" for this routing; Section 1 states that producing "the routing signal that any universal memory harness requires" was the motivating gap.

3. **A design rule: "trade read breadth for write depth"** — retrieve fewer entries per query and invest more in distilling/structuring memory at write time (Section 1, contributions).

4. **[Author-stated] Reproducibility:** code will be released upon acceptance (Abstract) — implicitly inviting further evaluation on the harness.

### Questions the verified claims leave unanswered but the authors do not mention

**[My inference]**

- **How should the router be built and evaluated?** The paper establishes that routing is necessary but does not specify the router's inputs (query features? predicted regime? measured attention?), how regime should be detected at deployment time without gold labels, or how routing errors should be priced.
- **Is a composed multi-substrate system actually better than its best constituent?** The paper never empirically constructs or tests a routed/composed system; the central recommendation is therefore a design hypothesis, not a verified result.
- **Do the conclusions survive other backbone families (especially GPT-family models, which dominate the field's evaluations per Figure 1b)?** Given the authors' own evidence of backbone dependence, extrapolation to GPT-family and frontier models is untested.
- **What happens beyond 262K tokens and in non-conflict long-horizon settings?** The scalability claim rests on one MAB sub-capability; other memory operations (e.g., long-range retrieval, accumulation, test-time learning) at 262K+ scale are not swept.
- **Can write-time distillation costs be amortized differently or made cheaper?** The Pareto analyses treat measured costs per benchmark run; the paper does not analyze regimes where one very long-lived agent performs enormous numbers of reads against one write phase, which would change which substrate is optimal.
- **How sensitive are the rankings to the auxiliary write model?** All write-side extraction, gisting, note evolution, and distillation used GPT-4O-MINI; no ablation varies the auxiliary model's capability, so it is unknown whether structural substrates' QA lead reflects the substrate or the adequacy of this particular extractor.
- **Why does M11 show inverse scaling between QWEN3-8B and QWEN3-32B-AWQ?** (Appendix E.1; Table 1.) The authors note the effect but do not diagnose whether it is an architecture interaction, misclustering, or a property of episode-clustered re-prefill at scale.

## (4) LOAD-BEARING ASSUMPTIONS

Assumptions on which the method or conclusions rely, and which newer evidence could invalidate. Each is **[my inference]** unless noted.

1. **Approximation fidelity: the paper's re-implementations represent their named substrate families.** The conclusion that "no substrate consistently dominates" is load-bearing on M8 and M11 being faithful enough proxies for MemSkill and EpiCache, and on M3/M5 loosely approximating Mem0/Zep. **[Author-stated in Appendix G/B.3 that deviations and loose approximations exist.]** New evidence from the actual production implementations (with RL controllers, kernel-level KV-cache edits, or full agent loops) could overturn per-substrate rankings.

2. **Fixed-harness validity: holding prompting, scaffolding, and auxiliary-LLM constant is the right way to isolate the substrate.** If certain substrates require co-adapted prompting/read protocols to perform (e.g., a learned controller for skill selection in M8, or kernel-level insertion in M11), then the harness itself may systematically disadvantage them. The assumption that a "fair" comparison requires identical scaffolding is never tested against a co-adapted-scaffolding comparison.

3. **Backbone representativeness: three open-weight models (8B, 32B, 26B-MoE) suffice to make regime-level claims.** The paper's own data show backbone-conditioned effects (e.g., "the value of raw-text retrieval on embodied planning is backbone-dependent," Section 4.2; M11 inverse scaling). New evidence from GPT-family, larger, or architecturally different models could invalidate both the specific winners and the claim that the QA-vs-agentic reversal is universal.

4. **Auxiliary model sufficiency: GPT-4O-MINI is competent enough at write-side extraction/distillation that substrate rankings reflect substrate structure rather than extraction quality.** This is load-bearing for M4/M5 (entity/relation extraction), M3 (gist fidelity), M7/M8 (strategy distillation and clustering), and M9 (synthetic QA-pair generation). A stronger or weaker auxiliary model could change which substrates benefit most.

5. **LLM-judge validity: P4 scores are treated as comparable measures of answer correctness across substrates.** Since the judge sees only (q, y, ŷ) and not the memory trace **[author-stated, Appendix D.2]**, style- or length-correlated judge preferences would propagate into the headline QA rankings. If judge rubrics, reference answers, or rubric ordering are shown to be unstable (the authors cite scoring-bias concerns in related-work discussion [86], Appendix F), the QA conclusions would require re-examination.

6. **Attention as explanation: last-token attention mass is a valid, decision-relevant proxy, and attention dilution is the cause (not mere correlate) of agentic degradation.** The entire "retrieval harms policy by shifting attention" narrative assumes that the binned attention regions ("System," "Retrieved," "Context," "Cue") correspond to the information the policy actually uses, and that observed shifts are causal. Mechanistic interpretability work showing that policies read irrelevant regions without performance loss, or that other heads/layers compensate, could weaken this conclusion.

7. **Benchmark-to-regime mapping: LoCoMo/LME/MAB CR instantiate "user-centric long-context QA," and ALFWorld/BCB-Hard instantiate "agent-centric sequential decision-making," well enough for the regime labels to generalize.** The user-centric benchmarks are overwhelmingly dialogue-based synthetic/benchmark histories; the agentic benchmarks are template-based embodied tasks and code tasks. Real deployment logs (long-horizon software agents, web navigation, personal companions across hundreds of sessions) could reveal different substrate trade-offs.

8. **No-memory baseline adequacy: the NoMem baseline (same template, empty retrieved block) controls for scaffolding effects.** If the prompt template itself was tuned for retrieval-augmented settings, NoMem may be artificially depressed, inflating apparent memory benefit. Conversely, if templates were tuned for NoMem, retrieval benefits could be understated.

9. **Scalability extrapolation: measured cost classes at 6K/32K/262K token regimes characterize longer horizons.** The claim that refinement substrates "hold their quality-per-second as histories grow" while structural and full-context substrates "face hard deployment limits" assumes these trends continue monotonically past 262K and across content types. New evidence at megatoken scale, or with incremental graph-update implementations (which would change M4/M5's rebuild cost profile), could alter the scalability taxonomy.

10. **Metric/undefined-cell conventions do not bias comparisons.** The decision to render undefined efficiency cells as "n/a" rather than zero (Appendix D.4) assumes that readers and downstream routing decisions will not misaggregate across substrates with different defined-metric sets — an assumption relevant to the claim of a unified 26-metric comparison.

11. **Taxonomy dependence.** The external/internal partition and the seven family labels are adopted from another survey (Huang et al. [19], cited in Sections 1–2). If that taxonomy misclassifies certain systems (e.g., KV-cache methods as "activation memory" vs "context engineering"), family-level statements inherit the error. The authors themselves find "family labels predict performance only weakly" (Section 5.1), which slightly undercuts family-level generalization but does not invalidate per-substrate findings.
