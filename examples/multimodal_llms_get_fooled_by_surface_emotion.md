# Research Brief: Multimodal LLMs Get Fooled by Surface Emotion — and We Want to Fix That

We have an ongoing multimodal sentiment analysis project, and somewhere along the way we noticed something that bugged us enough to want to study it properly. When a person's words and their actual emotional state don't line up — they say "I'm fine" but their face says otherwise — current multimodal LLMs almost always take the easy path. They trust whatever is on the surface, usually the literal text, fire off a confident response, and move on. "That's great to hear!" to someone who is clearly holding back tears. "I'm glad you're doing well!" to someone whose expression is sliding into distress right in front of the model.

This is not a corner case. It happens constantly once you start looking for it. Text-expression mismatches where the mouth says happy but the face is sad. Temporal drifts where someone starts composed and gradually shows discomfort. Sarcasm, self-deprecation, forced smiles. The model sails right through all of it with cheerful confidence, because it learned somewhere along the way that sounding sure of yourself is better than admitting you might be wrong about how someone is feeling.

We think this comes from how these models were trained after pretraining. The reward function during alignment probably rewards decisive, helpful-sounding answers and penalizes hedging. In most domains that makes sense. In emotion reading it is a problem, because the model would rather give a wrong answer with conviction than say "I'm not completely sure what you're feeling — can you tell me more?" Some closed-source models like GPT-5 already handle this noticeably better. They ask follow-up questions. They acknowledge uncertainty. They don't just pick the easiest signal and commit. We want to bring that same capability to open-source small models.

What we actually want to do is train open-source multimodal models to recognize when they don't have enough emotional signal to be confident, and instead of guessing wrong with full conviction, ei rewather ask the person what they're really feeling or express calibrated uncertainty. We want to do this through RL post-training with ard function that rewards honest uncertainty under genuine ambiguity while still rewarding confident answers when the signals are clear. The model should not become a hedging machine. It should know the difference between "I can read this clearly" and "I need more information."

On the data side, existing open-source emotion datasets almost never cover these cross-modal inconsistency cases, so we built our own synthetic data using LTX-2 for video generation and Z-Image for image generation. We deliberately chose synthetic data because it gives us precise control over the ground-truth emotional state — we know exactly what emotion we generated, so there is no guesswork about whether the model got it right or wrong. We generate scenarios where text and visual emotion are deliberately misaligned, scenarios where emotional state shifts over time, and scenarios with ironic or contradictory cues.

We also have a parallel research thread running: can we extract the real emotional signal from inside the model's own representations before it gets flattened into a confident but wrong final output? Sometimes the model might actually "know" the right answer internally but still output the wrong one because the training pushed it toward decisiveness. If we can pull out that internal signal, it could strengthen both the diagnosis and the fix. This might end up in the same paper or a separate one depending on how the results fall.

For models, we are looking at the Qwen family — Qwen-VL base and instruct versions. We know full well that base models have no post-training and instruct models have SFT and RLHF bundled together with no clean separation. That is fine for what we want to do. We are not trying to prove that RLHF caused the problem. We are trying to show that the problem exists in current post-trained models, and that RL post-training with a better reward design can fix it.

Hardware is four RTX 3090 cards or two A100 80GB cards or one RTX Pro 6000 96GB card. We are working within that.

The whole point of this project is simple: models should not confidently misread people's emotions just because the training taught them that sounding sure is more important than being right. We want to fix that, specifically in the multimodal emotion reading setting, using synthetic data with controlled conflicts, and RL post-training that teaches the model when to speak up and when to ask.
